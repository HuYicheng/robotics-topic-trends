# Semantic topic labels: checks (2026-10-28)

Generated by scripts/13b_semantic_validation.py. How the labels were made: data/semantic_labelling_prompt.md;
topic definitions: data/semantic_codebook_v2.md; labels: data/semantic_labels.csv.

## Stability

A random 2,320 papers were labelled a second time, in other batches by other agents. Agreement per topic
(Cohen's kappa) has median 0.97 and minimum 0.90; 78% of these papers got exactly
the same topics both times. This shows that the model is consistent with itself, not that it is right.

## Labels compared with the keyword rules

Keyword rules (data/topic_matrix_ta_keyword.csv): 72,364 paper-topic labels; semantic labels: 79,457; both: 55,035;
keyword only: 17,329; semantic only: 24,422. Per topic: results/semantic_vs_keyword_by_topic.csv.
Topics per paper (semantic, involves / main): 2020 3.05 / 1.89, 2021 3.06 / 1.89, 2022 3.17 / 1.93, 2023 3.27 / 1.95, 2024 3.43 / 2.03, 2025 3.64 / 2.10, 2026 4.24 / 2.17.

## Blind re-judgement of the disagreements

1,070 cells were judged again without saying which method said "yes": up to 8 per topic for each direction of
disagreement, and up to 3 per topic where the two semantic passes disagree. Share of each verdict (weighted by stratum size):

| cells | involves, words used | involves, other words | passing mention | other sense of the word | absent | unsure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| keyword only | 11% | 3% | 32% | 32% | 16% | 6% |
| semantic only | 46% | 51% | 0% | 1% | 0% | 2% |
| the two passes differ | 33% | 55% | 2% | 0% | 1% | 9% |

Scored against this re-judgement, the keyword rules have precision 0.80 and recall 0.71; the semantic labels 0.99 and 0.96
(per topic: results/semantic_score_by_topic.csv; 8 cases per stratum, so a single topic's numbers are rough: 8 of 8
right still has a 95% lower bound of about 0.63).

**What this does not show.** The re-judgement was made by the same model (Claude Opus 5.5) with the same codebook, and
it behaves much like labelling again: the semantic numbers measure the model's agreement with itself, not an
independent error rate. No human check has been made. Two more limits:
- Cells both methods miss are invisible here. The repeat pass shows some (226 cells where the keyword rules and the
  first pass say no and the second pass says yes; 90% of the 52 re-judged ones are yes): about 2,043 such
  cells in all papers, which caps recall at about 0.94 (semantic) and 0.69 (keyword).
- If the semantic-only cells were right less often than the re-judgement says, the semantic precision would still be
  0.97 at 90%, 0.94 at 80%, 0.91 at 70% (keyword rules: 0.80).

Yes-rate of the re-judged cells by period (weighted): keyword only 0.21 / 0.13 / 0.18; semantic only 0.96 / 0.99 / 0.97 (2020-22 / 2023-24 / 2025-26).
With 0-6 cases per topic and period, this cannot show whether a single topic's errors change over the years.
